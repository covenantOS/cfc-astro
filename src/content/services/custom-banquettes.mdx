## Construction and Fabric: What Makes a Banquette Last

A banquette gets sat on, slid across, and spilled on every day, so how it is built matters as much as how it looks. We use solid frames, proper foam density chosen for the seat (firmer for dining, where you want support, softer for a lounge nook), and finished upholstery with welting and zippered, removable covers where it makes sense. Fabric is the other half of the equation: for any kitchen or dining banquette we recommend performance fabrics like Crypton that resist stains and stand up to cleaning, drawn from the same Kravet and Stout libraries you can see on our [brands page](/brands/). For a formal or low-traffic banquette, the full designer range is open. We match the build and the fabric to how you will really use the seat.

## Getting the Dimensions Right

A banquette is only as good as its dimensions, and they are what separate a built-in you love from one nobody wants to sit at. Seat height matters most: too tall and feet dangle, too low and getting up is a chore, and the right height is set in relation to the table you will pair it with. Seat depth decides comfort and posture, deeper for a lounge-style nook where people linger, shallower and more upright for a dining banquette where you want to sit close to the table. The back angle, where there is a back, follows the same logic. Then there is clearance: enough room to slide in and out comfortably, and a table positioned so everyone can reach without climbing over a neighbor. These are the measurements we work out at the consultation, against your actual room and the table you have or plan to buy. The same care goes into how the window above it is dressed, whether that means [plantation shutters](/services/plantation-shutters/) or a soft shade sized to the bay. A banquette that looks perfect but sits wrong is a daily annoyance, while one built to the right numbers becomes the seat everyone fights for.
