## What Are Cornices and Valances, And What's the Difference

These two terms get used interchangeably, but they're different:

Cornices are rigid, box-style top treatments, typically constructed from wood or MDF with a decorative facing and often covered in fabric. They mount directly above the window and create a strong architectural line. Cornices work particularly well in rooms with traditional or transitional aesthetics, or in spaces where you want a deliberate, structured finish to the window.

Valances are softer, fabric-based top treatments that hang or drape across the top of the window. They can be straight, shaped, or swagged, and they tend to feel lighter and less formal than cornices. Valances are a good choice when you want the finishing touch without the full architectural weight of a cornice, or when you want to add a fabric element that coordinates with drapery panels below.

Both are custom, and both solve the same core problem: the top of the window needs to look finished, not like an afterthought.

## Why Tampa Bay Windows Often Need Custom Top Treatments

Florida homes have a window problem that doesn't exist in most other markets.

Concrete block construction, vaulted ceilings, off-standard window heights, and hurricane-impact windows all create situations where standard top treatments don't fit. The window sits higher than the trim. The mounting bracket for the blinds doesn't have anywhere clean to attach. The ceiling angle creates a gap that retail top treatments can't bridge.

Custom cornices and valances solve this because they're built to your exact measurements and installed to work with your specific window, not adapted around it.

Additionally, the humidity and temperature swings in Tampa Bay affect the materials used in top treatments. A poorly constructed cornice can sag over time. We build with materials and construction methods that hold up to Florida conditions.

## Types of Custom Top Treatments We Build in Tampa Bay

Cornices, Box-style rigid top treatments available in a range of heights and projections. We build them in wood or MDF with fabric facing, and offer different facing profiles depending on the look you want. Cornices are mounted directly to the wall above the window, creating a clean architectural line that works in both traditional and contemporary spaces.

Fabric valances, Draped or shaped fabric treatments mounted on a board or batten. Available in a range of styles: straight edge, patterned, shaped, or with returns that wrap around to the wall. Fabric valances coordinate naturally with drapery panels below and add softness to the top of the window without the full weight of a cornice.

Swagged and shaped valances, More decorative valance styles with cascading fabric or geometric shapes. These work best in formal living rooms, dining rooms, or spaces where the window treatment is a focal point of the room's design.

Coordinated drapery top treatments, When we design a full drapery system, we typically design the top treatment as part of the overall specification, matching the fabric, the heading style, and the proportions so everything reads as one intentional design decision.
