## Composite Shutters: What They Are and When They Win

Composite sits between faux wood and basswood, and it is the material most people have not heard of when they start shopping. The core is an engineered wood substrate, and it is wrapped in a hard polymer or vinyl skin. That construction gives it most of the moisture resistance of faux wood with a stiffer panel, so it spans wider openings without the sag you get when a heavy vinyl shutter gets too large.

Where composite earns its place in a St. Pete home is the room that is humid but also visible. A faux wood shutter is the obvious answer for a small bathroom window nobody sees. In a kitchen that opens onto the living room, or a bath with a wide window facing the water, composite gives you the same tolerance for steam and salt air with a cleaner edge profile and a finish that reads closer to painted wood.

The tradeoffs are honest ones. Composite costs more than faux wood and less than basswood. It is heavier than real wood, so on a very tall opening we still spec basswood to keep the hinges happy. And it comes in fewer stain options, because the skin is finished rather than the grain. When we walk your house we tell you which rooms are faux wood rooms, which are composite rooms, and which are worth doing in basswood, rather than quoting the whole house in one material.
